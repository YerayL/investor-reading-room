David Booth: Why I’m Still Working

第 1 段Once I hit 75, people started asking me when I was going to retire. “What do you mean?” I’d respond. “I am retired. This is my retirement!”

第 2 段So why do I choose to keep coming into the office every day, even after running my firm for more than 40 years? Because the ideas upon which we founded the firm are bigger and more important than the firm itself, and I’ve made it my life’s mission to share this information with as many people as possible.

第 3 段Now don’t turn away; this isn’t a sales pitch. I’m not going to offer you the “opportunity” to make any sort of investment that benefits me or my company. Instead, I’m going to endorse a practice utilized by many wealthy people for years: the enormous possibility and potential unlocked by investing in the stock market.

第 4 段The Stock Market Is for Everyone

第 5 段The stock market may seem like a club for rich folks, but you don’t need an invitation to get in. This myth of exclusivity prevents too many people from realizing their dreams. Anyone can invest, whether you have $100 or $1 million.

第 6 段The stock market is the rare meeting place where two parties on opposite sides of the same transaction can both benefit. A buyer doesn’t buy a share of stock unless he or she feels good about the price, and the same is true for the seller. Win-win.

第 7 段Maybe you think the stock market isn’t a good place to put your money because you think it’s about picking stocks or timing the market. To me, that’s not what investing is about. The evidence that an individual can do better than the market is pretty sketchy.

第 8 段When I refer to “investing in the stock market,” I mean one specific strategy: buying shares in a mutual fund or ETF that offers a broad representation of the whole stock market. Since this provides a high level of diversification, you’re protected against the risk of being overexposed in any one type of industry or company.

第 9 段To be sure, investing comes with uncertainty. But by taking on that uncertainty, investors have historically earned higher returns over time.

第 10 段Power of Compounding

第 11 段And you’ll want to embrace that uncertainty as soon as you can in order to benefit from a potent tool available to all: time. The longer you invest in the stock market, the better.

第 12 段This means you only need to invest $5,000 in the stock market at age 21 to potentially accrue more than $750,000 by age 75. That’s without contributing any additional funds over the course of your lifetime.

第 13 段Let’s say you’re not 21 anymore—most of us aren’t—and you don’t have as much time. Maybe you’ve written off the idea of retirement altogether. Maybe you’re really worried about the future. If you can find $100,000 to put into the stock market at age 50 (again, I know this isn’t an easy task), you could be a millionaire before your 75th birthday.

第 14 段You have probably experienced the power of compounding in your own life; through patience and commitment, you’ve enjoyed small wins (whether at work or at home) that have led to long-term rewarding outcomes that you may have not thought possible at the start of your journey. The same principle applies to investing.

第 15 段That’s the miracle of compound returns.

第 16 段Believe in the Economy

第 17 段Now let’s talk about why I think the economy is always biased in your favor. Because people want to make their lives better! So they do clever things, even during tough times, to advance even faster.

第 18 段That means you don’t have to do any research or entertain any opinion—when you hold a diversified portfolio all you have to believe in is the power of human ingenuity. As long as you accept that the total economy is likely to keep growing over time—and over the past 100 years, it has—you can get behind this strategy. The total market value can still grow even when one company or sector performs poorly.

第 19 段When you take a step back and look at long-term historical returns, the total value of the stock market has gone up over time, regardless of political administrations or the general state of the world. Stock prices can fluctuate wildly in the short term but, collectively, tend to rise over time—because human beings, and the businesses they create, are resilient.

第 20 段Why do I think the stock market will continue to be the smartest investment going forward? Economists spend their time developing models of stock market returns and then testing them out. The big risk comes from stuff that’s outside the model. Several times over the past hundred years, the outlook seemed pretty risky. The Great Depression, World War II, the crash of ’87, the Great Recession, the pandemic—all those events frightened people and may have seemed insurmountable. But human beings don’t just sit there and accept adversity—they figure out what it takes to get back on track.

第 21 段That’s my pitch: Invest in a diversified portfolio, hold it for as long as you can, and believe in a growing economy. You don’t have to chase flashy stocks or trends to have a successful investing experience. Instead, you can give yourself the gift of a better future by putting whatever you can into the market today.

第 22 段Soon, you’ll be the one who can’t stop talking about the market.
