CFO playbook: Five elements of building world-class investor relations

第 1 段Jeff Epstein & Alex Wu, CFO Advisors

第 2 段Published on May 6, 2025

第 3 段As a founder or CFO racing to build your company while putting out the latest fires, investor communications often fall to the bottom of the priority list. While prioritizing the latest product launch, customer calls, and hiring (rightfully so), investor communications can become reactive for founders and CEOs; especially in the early stages of a company, these can be hastily assembled updates sent only when absolutely necessary, rather than leveraged as a strategic advantage.

第 4 段Easy as it is to slip into, a reactive approach creates an existential risk for your company. In today's capital-competitive environment, how well your narrative resonates with investors directly determines not just your valuation, but whether or not you can secure funding at all. Companies with strategic investor relations command premium valuations and maintain funding options even in downturns, while those without a deliberate approach often find themselves unable to create interest with investors when capital access matters most.

第 5 段"The most successful companies provide enough visibility that investors know if you're executing against the narrative you sold them on," explains Bryan Vaniman, SVP Corporate Development, Investor Relations and Treasury at Twilio. This insight cuts to the heart of effective investor relations—it's not about overwhelming detail, but rather providing views from the right altitude to validate your strategic story.

第 6 段This CFO playbook breaks down world-class investor relations into five essential elements that CFOs and founders can master to secure investor interest in any economic climate.

第 7 段CFO Playbook: Investor relations overview

第 8 段Core roles & responsibilities

第 9 段Crafting your strategic market narrative

第 10 段Are you controlling your company's narrative, or is the market creating it for you? Every company has a story in the market—whether you're actively shaping it or not. Without deliberate narrative management, investors and analysts will create their own version of your story, often missing crucial elements that drive long-term value.

第 11 段The best founders and CFOs don't just report metrics—they weave those numbers into a compelling narrative about where the company is headed and why it matters. This narrative-metrics integration creates a virtuous cycle: your story makes your numbers meaningful, and your numbers validate your story.

第 12 段The foundation of effective IR (investor relations) is a compelling, consistent narrative that investors not only understand but can also advocate for in your absence.

第 13 段Creating a narrative that investors can repeat with conviction

第 14 段How do you know if your story is landing effectively? One powerful technique is to directly test comprehension during investor meetings. After presenting your story, ask the investor to articulate it back to you in their own words. This exercise immediately reveals whether your key messages are being understood and internalized.

第 15 段Claroty CFO Udi Bar Sela recalls once being 45 minutes into a meeting with an analyst and saying, “We have 15 minutes left—let’s do this: I want to hear you tell our story back to me.”

第 16 段"Just by hearing him speak,” Udi of Claroty remembers, “I could tell the message was well-received because the way he described it was perfect."

第 17 段Practical test: After your next investor meeting, ask yourself: "If I asked this investor to pitch our company thesis back to me, would they be just as convincing as our team?" If not, your narrative needs refinement.

第 18 段Building your amplification network

第 19 段Your direct investor communications are only one piece of the narrative puzzle. The real firepower comes from building relationships with influencers who amplify your story.

第 20 段- Analysts as educators: Identify key analysts within your sector, build relationships with them, and ensure they deeply understand your narrative. These analysts educate other market participants about your company, creating a multiplier effect for your messaging.

第 21 段- Strategic investors as advocates: Build relationships with long-term investors who really get your business so they will become powerful advocates. These investors speak to other investors, reinforce your narrative in their networks, and provide stability during volatile periods.

第 22 段Treating investor relations like strategic sales

第 23 段One of the most transformative approaches to IR is treating the function with the same discipline and rigor as you do with sales. Bryan of Twilio shares how this shift created measurable results:

第 24 段"We've been much more programmatic each quarter about targeting folks who could be our next cohort of long-term investors," he explains. "We’re treating it like a sales job where you have to build a pipeline."

第 25 段This approach transforms investor relations from a reactive function to a proactive strategy with clear metrics:

第 26 段- Target identification: Identify potential investors whose investment thesis aligns with your evolving business model and growth stage.

第 27 段- Proactive outreach: Regularly communicate with targeted investors rather than relying solely on inbound interest.

第 28 段- Long-term relationship building: Invest time in developing relationships, recognizing that investor education and building trust are prerequisites to eventually securing funding.

第 29 段- Conversion tracking: Monitor which investor meetings convert into actual investments, creating accountability metrics that speak to the effectiveness of your targeting and narrative.

第 30 段How success is measured

第 31 段Creating metrics that drive valuation

第 32 段Effective IR isn't just about pushing information outward—it's about creating feedback loops that bring valuable market intelligence back into your company. In other words, external awareness drives internal insight. When done right, your IR function becomes a competitive advantage by helping you understand how the market perceives your strategy, your competition, and your industry.

第 33 段Specific metrics for measuring IR effectiveness

第 34 段Based on insights from experienced IR leaders, these metrics separate world-class programs from merely adequate ones:

第 35 段Quantitative success metrics:

第 36 段- Target engagement rate: Establish specific numerical targets for engaging your top investors each quarter (e.g., your goal could be to engage with 80% of your top 10 ideal investors in a given quarter)

第 37 段- Investor conversion tracking: Move beyond activity metrics to measure how many targeted meetings convert to actual investment decisions (e.g., your goal could be to convert 5% of your meetings into investments.)

第 38 段- Predictive accuracy: Track what percentage of investor questions were anticipated versus coming as surprises during earnings calls or meetings. (e.g., your goal could be to have less than 10% of questions come as a surprise.)

第 39 段- New investor acquisition: Measure the success of proactive outreach by tracking new investors gained through targeted versus inbound channels. (E.g., your goal could be to gain 50% of investors through outbound channels.)

第 40 段Qualitative success metrics:

第 41 段- Narrative penetration: Assess whether investors can accurately repeat your company’s market thesis without prompting or misinterpretation.

第 42 段- Question evolution: Monitor the progression from basic business model questions toward sophisticated strategic discussions.

第 43 段- Feedback loop effectiveness: Evaluate how effectively investor insights are translated into actionable intelligence for executive decisions.

第 44 段- Crisis resilience: Measure investor retention and valuation premium during challenging periods.

第 45 段- Executive narrative alignment: Audit the consistency of messaging across different executives using mock interviews.

第 46 段Discerning which feedback matters most

第 47 段Gaining value from investor interactions requires you to distinguish between valuable feedback and market noise.

第 48 段"A big part of the role is ensuring you’re effective at creating a feedback loop. Are you taking all the insights and comments and feedback from investors and relaying that back to both the executive team and the board? Are you making active decisions and changes based on feedback you’re getting?" — Bryan Vaniman

第 49 段Not all investor feedback requires action. The art of effective IR is distinguishing between:

第 50 段- Misconceptions that require better education: When investors misunderstand your business model or strategy, the solution lies in improving education, not making strategic changes.

第 51 段- Valid concerns that deserve attention: When multiple sophisticated investors raise similar concerns about strategic direction or execution, taking this feedback seriously can be invaluable.

第 52 段Case study: How Twilio turned investor concerns into a display of the business’s strength

第 53 段When Twilio’s blended gross margin slipped from the high-50s to the low-50 percent range, investor concerns mounted—fearing that it could signal anything from eroding pricing power to increasing costs to serve. Instead of offering vague assurances, the company used its Investor Day to tell the full story.

第 54 段First, it disentangled the numbers—showing that the mix was tilting toward SMS and WhatsApp messaging, a product line that naturally carries lower percentage margins than pure-software subscriptions but drives far more absolute contribution dollars. Second, it unpacked geography: international traffic incurs higher carrier pass-through fees than U.S. traffic, which drags the reported percentage down even though per-message pricing was rising year over year. By laying out unit economics product-by-product and region-by-region, management reframed the “margin decline” as evidence that lucrative messaging volume and global expansion were accelerating exactly as planned — using this as an opportunity to turn investor skepticism into confidence.

第 55 段Common failure modes

第 56 段Preserving credibility when challenges arise

第 57 段How you handle challenges often determines investor confidence more than how you deliver good news. That’s why it’s critical to approach these conversations transparently yet skillfully—and with the appropriate depth of understanding.

第 58 段"Investors can smell the difference between someone who truly understands the business versus someone who's just relaying talking points." —Sameer Dholakia, former CEO of SendGrid and current Partner at Bessemer

第 59 段According to the IR experts we interviewed, there are three critical errors that consistently undermine investor confidence:

第 60 段- Inconsistent messaging: When your CTO paints one picture at a conference and the CFO presents different information to investors, this creates confusion and suspicion. Udi of Claroty notes, "Confidence gets shaken when there is inconsistency between different parts in the organization."

第 61 段- Reactive rather than proactive communication: Waiting until problems are unavoidable before acknowledging them makes you appear either unaware or dishonest. Bryan of Twilio advises, "Don't wait until you miss your quarterly earnings guidance. Don't wait until an upset investor shows up at the door. Anticipate the situations that could happen and make sure you've got a plan of action."

第 62 段- Surface-level understanding: Sameer emphasizes that IR professionals often fail when they "are not super embedded into the business." When tough questions arise, this lack of depth becomes obvious and damages credibility.

第 63 段Balancing transparency with narrative control

第 64 段How do you provide sufficient transparency while maintaining narrative control? The secret lies in providing metrics at the right altitude—giving investors enough visibility to track your execution without drowning them in detail:

第 65 段- Core quarterly metrics: Select a focused set of indicators that directly validate your strategic narrative. These metrics should provide the right vantage point to answer the question: "Are we executing against our stated strategy?" Review this set of metrics annually to ensure they still align with your evolving priorities.

第 66 段- Annual deep dives: Provide context through periodic analysis that connects operational details to strategic outcomes. This cadence offers deeper visibility into specific areas like product performance without the effort of quarterly reporting cycles.

第 67 段- Investor day strategic engagements: Use investor days to present comprehensive frameworks that reinforce your narrative with supporting data. These events allow you to share deeper insights without committing to ongoing detailed disclosures that could create quarter-to-quarter noise and unnecessary scrutiny.

第 68 段This layered approach to disclosure ensures investors have enough visibility to know if you're executing against the narrative you sold them on, without creating information overload. Finding this balance is crucial—too little information undermines credibility, but too much detail obscures the strategic storyline and creates unnecessary volatility from quarter-to-quarter.

第 69 段How the role evolves across growth stages

第 70 段Evolving from founder-led to independent strategic function

第 71 段The right IR resources at the right stage of your company are a key piece of elevating IR into a sustainable competitive advantage.

第 72 段The founder as the first IR professional

第 73 段At early stages of building a company, the CEO and CFO are the investor relations department. This is not only efficient but often necessary—early investors want direct access to founders, and no one can tell the company story better than its creators.

第 74 段But as your company grows, so do the demands of investor communications. Different stages require different IR capabilities, and understanding this evolution helps you optimize resources while maximizing valuation impact.

第 75 段Growth stage to post-IPO: the critical transition

第 76 段As fundraising becomes more complex and company operations more sophisticated, IR requirements grow. This transition point often determines whether companies build sustainable capital market advantages or struggle with misaligned investor expectations. "We hired our IR person as a full-time employee a year before we went public,” shares Sameer when reflecting on his time as CEO of Sendgrid. “I wanted him to sit in our operating meetings every Monday for a year, attend our quarterly business reviews, and hear from every product leader and sales leader to really understand the business."

第 77 段While this investment might seem premature, it paid dividends. "It was a big investment,” Sameer acknowledges. “For a while, the person we hired was probably 20% productive, 80% learning. But that mattered to me so that once we were public, he knew our business really well. When he spoke to analysts and investors, he had credibility because he deeply understood the business and knew our numbers."

第 78 段The investor relations function evolution: A strategic roadmap

第 79 段Impact of technology & AI

第 80 段Using AI to enhance intelligence while managing risk

第 81 段Technology is transforming investor relations, but it requires strategic implementation. "The key is identifying where technology truly enhances the human relationship aspects of IR versus where it creates risk," explains Sameer, who has guided multiple companies through technology transformations.

第 82 段IR leaders must balance efficiency with precision, especially when handling sensitive financial information. Rather than rushing to implement new tools across all IR functions, the most effective approach is targeting specific high-value, lower-risk applications.

第 83 段High-impact, low-risk applications

第 84 段Despite appropriate caution around using AI to process sensitive data, three major AI applications are emerging as particularly valuable for IR professionals:

第 85 段1. Earnings preparation acceleration

第 86 段CFOs and IR teams typically spend days preparing for earnings calls, reviewing dozens of pages of scripts, metrics, and potential questions. Leading IR teams use AI to condense and analyze this information. For example, teams use AI to streamline content that might be four pages into something more concise for our executives. This efficiency means we can spend more time on strategic thinking rather than manual review.”

第 87 段2. Competitive intelligence synthesis

第 88 段Rather than manually analyzing dozens of research reports, AI can quickly identify common themes and concerns across your industry. "That's been super helpful," notes Bryan of Twilio, "because it gives you insights like, 'Common issues analysts are citing are macro or sector-specific concerns. We should be prepared to answer that question.'" This advance warning system allows you to prepare for likely investor questions before you’re put on the spot to answer.

第 89 段3. Narrative alignment detection

第 90 段IR effectiveness depends on consistent messaging, yet analysts often develop their own interpretations of your story. AI excels at comparing these external narratives against your intended messaging. As many experienced IR professionals explain: "AI can read research reports about your company to quickly identify who has the wrong narrative. You can prompt it to uncover the five to eight bullet point narrative summary that they think they're underwriting, and compare it to how it differs from your internal messaging." This capability helps you identify and correct misalignments before they spread.

第 91 段"The human element remains irreplaceable in investor relations. Technology should enhance relationship-building and insight gathering, not replace the trust that comes from personal connection." - Sameer Dholakia

第 92 段The strategic value of investor relations excellence

第 93 段If there's one resounding theme that emerges from our conversations with top IR leaders, it's that investor relations has evolved far beyond simple financial reporting. The best practitioners approach IR as a strategic function that directly impacts company valuation, capital access, and long-term growth.

第 94 段Regardless of your company's stage, the journey to world-class investor relations begins with the same foundational principle: crafting a compelling, consistent narrative that investors can understand, believe in, and repeat to others. From this narrative core, you can build the measurement systems, crisis protocols, organizational capabilities, and technology infrastructure that elevate your IR function from reactive reporting to strategic advantage. Next time you need to raise a round of funding, you’ll be glad you did.
